Every candidate contesting an election in India must file a sworn affidavit
(Form 26) disclosing their **criminal cases, assets, liabilities, income and
education**. These are public documents. Here's how to find and read them.

## Where to find affidavits

1. **Election Commission of India (ECI)** — the official source. Affidavits for
   each election are published on the ECI website.
2. **MyNeta.info** — run by the non-profit ADR, this puts affidavit data into a
   searchable, easy-to-read format. Useful for quick lookups; for anything
   important, cross-check against the original ECI affidavit.

## How to read the criminal-cases section

The affidavit lists:
- **Pending cases** — cases where charges are framed but not decided. Remember:
  pending ≠ guilty.
- **Convictions** — cases where a court has already found the person guilty.

Note the **section of law** cited and whether the case is *pending* or
*convicted*. A long list of pending cases is worth scrutiny, but it is not the
same as proven wrongdoing.

## How to read the assets & liabilities section

- **Movable and immovable assets** for the candidate, spouse and dependents.
- **Liabilities** such as loans.
- Compare with **previous affidavits** (from earlier elections) to spot
  unusual jumps in wealth — a common starting point for accountability stories.

## A simple verification checklist

1. Open the original ECI affidavit (PDF), not just a summary.
2. Note pending vs. convicted cases separately.
3. Check the **self-declared** nature of the data — it's sworn, but it's still
   the candidate's own declaration.
4. Compare across years if earlier affidavits exist.
5. Save/screenshot the source and note the date — always keep your evidence.

This single document is the most powerful free tool an Indian voter has. Learn
to read it and you can verify most "is this politician corrupt?" questions
yourself.
