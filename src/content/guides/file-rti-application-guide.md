The Right to Information (RTI) Act, 2005 lets any Indian citizen ask most
public authorities for information — and they're legally bound to answer. It's
one of the most powerful tools for citizen accountability. Here's how to use it.

## Who can file, and what it costs

- **Any citizen of India** can file.
- The standard fee is small (often ₹10; some states differ). People below the
  poverty line are usually exempt.

## How to file

You can file in two ways:

1. **Online** via the central RTI portal (for central-government bodies) or your
   state's RTI portal.
2. **On paper**, addressed to the **Public Information Officer (PIO)** of the
   relevant department, by post or in person.

## Writing a good RTI application

Keep it specific and factual:

- Address it to the **PIO** of the correct department.
- Ask **clear, answerable questions** ("Provide copies of sanctioned tender
  documents for project X between dates A and B").
- Avoid vague or opinion-based questions ("Why is the department corrupt?") —
  the Act covers *information*, not opinions or explanations.
- Request **copies of documents** where possible; they're harder to fudge.

## Time limits

The PIO must usually reply within **30 days** (48 hours where life or liberty
is involved).

## If you're refused or ignored

- **First appeal:** to the First Appellate Authority within the same body.
- **Second appeal:** to the **State or Central Information Commission**.

## Using RTI responsibly for reporting

RTI replies are excellent primary sources because they come straight from the
authority. When you publish something based on RTI:
- Keep the original reply and the application.
- Quote the document accurately.
- Don't overstate — report exactly what the record says.
